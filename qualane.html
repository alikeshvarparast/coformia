    Coverage before the shift starts

    Every running line has a name on it.

    The manager assigns technicians to lines. One person can cover several lines. Open tasks, holds, and yellow retests carry into the next shift with a handover note. Missed tasks are logged. They are not deleted.

    Automated coding

    Nobody types the code at midnight.

    At rollover the engine builds the next code, sends it to the coder, and confirms the active message matches. If the coder cannot be reached, the technician sees the correct code, enters it, and scans a verification sample. Every change is logged.

    Pilot

    One line. One product.

    Qualane is new. The first pilot proves the loop: assign, sample with proof, enter results, flag, and print a batch record. Coding hardware, vision, and ERP come after that loop holds.

    1. Bring the paper. Two or three real forms, test lists, and spec limits.
    2. Run one shift on a tablet. NFC or a phone camera is enough for proof of sampling.
    3. Measure it. Lab-report time cut, every sample with proof, technicians who prefer it to paper.
    4. Then the coder. Gateway, read-back, and optional line stop stay a phase-two choice.

    Questions

    What does Qualane do on a shift?

    It turns a QC technician’s shift into a timed task list, requires proof that each sample was taken, pre-fills lab forms, and flags results against the product spec as they are entered.

    Does Qualane replace the lab or the ERP?

    No. Technicians still enter or import results. Holds and releases stay with the Head of QC. Instrument import and ERP connections are later phases, shared with Formulane where the plant already has that path.

    Who can release product after a red result?

    Only the Head of QC, with a reason and a signature. A red result holds pallets from that lot since the last green result. AI can suggest a cause. It cannot change a status, release product, or sign anything.

    What standards is the record built for?

    Safe Food for Canadians, HACCP and preventive controls, and GFSI schemes such as SQF and BRCGS. Electronic signatures are designed so they can meet FDA 21 CFR Part 11 for plants that export. Records are corrected with a reason. They are not deleted.
